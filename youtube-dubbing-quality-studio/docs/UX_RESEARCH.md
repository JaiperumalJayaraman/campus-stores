# UX research plan · Language Lab

**Research status: Not conducted.** All shown segments and confidence estimates are synthetic. Do not present this as completed user research.

## Study design

Recruit 5–8 bilingual video creators/editors who have reviewed translated media. Target a spread of education, gaming, technology and general content; avoid relying exclusively on friends. Explain that the tool is an independent concept.

## Questions

1. Describe the last translation mistake you caught in a video. What did you do to fix it?
2. Which mistakes matter most: names, timing, jargon, tone, or safety?
3. Show me how you currently decide whether a dub is acceptable.
4. Who has permission to approve a translation in your workflow?
5. Would you prefer a direct correction or regeneration option? Why?

## Usability tasks

- T1: Identify the segment containing “gradient descent”. **Success:** find S3 unaided.
- T2: Correct and save the translation. **Success:** correction persists after navigation and refresh.
- T3: Approve a segment and then reopen it. **Success:** completion changes correctly.
- T4: Download and inspect corrected SRT text. **Success:** exported file includes correction.

Measure: task success (binary), task duration (seconds), wrong clicks, time-to-first-correction, SEQ ease rating (1–7), qualitative participant quotes. Gather consent; omit personally identifying information from public repo.

## Accessibility review

Keyboard navigation and visible focus; color plus status text; translated language legibility; large text/long captions; mobile overflow and scalable layouts. Check with screen readers before production.
